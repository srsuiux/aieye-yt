## The videos you make

You make the videos with AI models from other companies. Who owns AI-made work, and how it can be used, depends on the
companies' terms and on the law where you live, and both are still changing. In general:

- Read the terms of OpenAI and of each video model's company for what you may do with their output, including commercial use.
- Copyright law in some countries gives little or no protection to work made only by AI. Do not assume you own the videos
  exclusively, or that no one can copy them.
- The authors of AI Eye claim no rights over the videos, pictures, scripts or voice you make with it.

## Things to check before you publish

- **Facts.** Scripts are written by AI and can contain mistakes. AI Eye asks for well-known facts and has a quality check, but
  it does not verify them. You are responsible for what you publish.
- **Other people's work.** Do not paste copyrighted text, images, music or someone's likeness into topics, notes or prompts
  and publish the result. Do not ask for images of real people, logos or characters that belong to others.
- **Real people and brands.** Pictures that look real can mislead. Do not use AI Eye to imitate a real person.
- **Narration.** The voices are synthetic voices from OpenAI. Say so where a platform asks you to.
- **AI labels.** YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic content that is altered or made with AI. AI Eye does not set that
  label for you when it uploads. Set it yourself in YouTube Studio after the upload if your video needs it.
- **Music and sounds.** AI Eye adds no music. If you add your own, you need the right to use it.

## Taking something down

If you believe something made with this software, or code in this repository, infringes your rights, open an issue on the
project's GitHub page or contact the repository owner there. For a video on YouTube, use YouTube's own copyright process.
